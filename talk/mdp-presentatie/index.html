From robots to polders: world models in hydrology

A polder has to get through days of heavy rain and a summer with almost none. You can retain water on the land, dig open water, buy a bigger pumping station, or let water in from outside. Which mix works, and what does it cost? A forecast does not tell you. For that you need a model that shows what your choices do — in robotics that is called a world model. Ours has no neural network: rules of thumb and a water balance, checkable on the back of an envelope.
